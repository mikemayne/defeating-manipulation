Real-world argument corpus

Collect genuine, continuous passages from published writing. Label each passage before seeing the analyser's output, then export the JSON and run it in the Matcher Test Lab. Excerpts and notes stay on your computer; this page sends nothing to external services.

Sampling protocol

  1. Natural sample: take consecutive paragraphs from varied genuine reports, interviews, news and commentary, whether or not you expect an argument. This measures false positives and actual coverage.
  2. Challenge sample: independently select passages with an identifiable conclusion, without adding wording or inventing premises. Include genuinely supported, insufficiently supported and contradicted conclusions.
  3. Copy the source verbatim, with enough preceding sentences to preserve relevant context. Prefer one main inference per case. A conclusion may be implicit; label the real argument, not what the current parser happens to recognise.
  4. If the correct label is unclear, mark Uncertain / exclude and do not include it in the scored export. Record your rationale before looking at model predictions.

“Unsupported” means that the supplied evidence does not establish the conclusion; it does not mean the conclusion is false. A report of two people disagreeing is not automatically a contradiction in the article.
