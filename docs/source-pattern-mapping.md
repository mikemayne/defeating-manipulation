# Mapping the Surviving a Psyop catalogue to online article patterns

This document records the review of the pattern chapters in [surviving-a-psyop](https://github.com/mikemayne/surviving-a-psyop). It distinguishes patterns that can be identified in the language of an online article from interpersonal, physical, technical or multi-event patterns that cannot be established from one article's text.

The mapping is about whether a comparable **textual pattern** exists. It does not transfer assumptions about how or why an event in the source guide occurred.

## Related patterns not imported as standalone article-text techniques

These chapters describe interactions, conditions or communication dynamics that may be meaningful in other settings but are not reliably identifiable from the words of a single online article:

- **Interpersonal dynamics:** Gaslighting by friends; good cop/bad cop; Stockholm syndrome; habituation; emotional burden; induced sympathy as a staged interaction; false assistance; service shield; pretending to be overheard; pretend planning; presence threat; humiliation rituals; bodily-function harassment; and private/public behaviour contrasts.
- **Physical or coercive conditions:** Sleep deprivation; deprivation of basic necessities; sexual coercion; threats to loved ones; indefinite abuse; and cumulative pressure across repeated encounters. These require information about conduct or a sequence of events, not just article wording.
- **Technology and perception:** Dream manipulation; cold reading and AI-assisted cold reading; prediction of bodily movements; source identification; internal monologue; thought attribution; suggested mishearings; quiet speech and masking; spatial audio; bug-in-face; compromised devices; air-gap limitations; and privacy. These are not article-language patterns. Some media manipulation can be analysed as a separate image or video task, but not by the text-only catalogue.
- **Organisational or legal process:** Delayed organisational responses; legal intimidation; references to the Official Secrets Act; false-report threats; and consequences for seeking help. An article may discuss these subjects, but the process itself is not a textual technique.
- **Cross-platform or multi-event patterns:** Flashmobs/brigading, repeated pressure across many encounters, and coordinated activity across multiple channels require evidence beyond a single article. A future corpus-level analysis could examine repeated language across multiple sources, but it should be a separate capability.

## Research basis

The new entries use established work on propaganda detection, online news framing and rhetorical techniques. The research supports the general approach of identifying specific textual spans and explaining the technique. The more specific labels in this mapping are descriptive extensions where the source guide's pattern has a clear online-language counterpart.
