# Analysis design

## Purpose
The analysis system helps a reader inspect the language and structure of an online article. It identifies candidate textual patterns and explains why they may matter.

## Unit of analysis
Work with a selected passage and enough surrounding context to interpret it. Keep paragraph boundaries and source positions so every finding can be traced back to the page.

## Output for each candidate
- Technique identifier and human-readable name.
- Exact supporting quotation.
- Location in the source text where available.
- Short explanation of the textual feature.
- Confidence that the passage matches the technique.
- Alternative interpretation or contextual caveat.
- Links to the catalogue definition and references.

## Separate three questions
1. What can be observed in the text?
2. Which recognised concept might describe that feature?
3. What effect might that feature have on a reader?

Do not collapse these into claims about the author's intention. Intent cannot reliably be inferred from a text alone.

## Evidence standards
A finding must cite a specific passage. A generic statement such as "this article is propaganda" is not an acceptable output. Distinguish a direct quotation from a paraphrase. Do not invent source context, references or facts.

## Analysis stages
1. Extract the reader-selected text locally.
2. Segment into paragraphs and sentences while retaining offsets.
3. Use deterministic matching for simple, well-defined patterns where appropriate.
4. Ask the local model to propose candidate passages and technique IDs from the approved catalogue.
5. Validate model output against the catalogue schema.
6. Display only findings with traceable evidence and an explanation.
7. Let the reader inspect, dismiss or correct findings.

The model is an assistant for finding candidates, not an authority. It should be allowed to return no finding. It must not be rewarded for maximising the number of techniques detected.
