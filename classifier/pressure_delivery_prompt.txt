Apply only these independent labels:
0. Shame or guilt. Uses moral failure, disloyalty, shame or indebtedness as leverage for a choice or concession.
1. Fear of consequences. Uses a threatened or implied penalty, harm or retaliation to push a choice. Ordinary risk warnings and descriptions of danger lack this use of leverage.
2. Doubt about one's own judgement. Dismisses a person's perception, memory or understanding in a way that pressures them to distrust it. Disagreement or a specific correction backed by evidence is a no-match for this label.
3. Identity or credibility pressure. Uses a person's character, reputation, identity or reaction to discredit them, their claim or their ability to be believed. An insult alone is insufficient.
4. Loss of agency. Frames meaningful choice as unavailable, compliance as the only escape or every offered response as punishable. A neutral constraint or an ordinary choice is insufficient.
5. Insufficient context. Use alone where a missing fact materially prevents deciding whether any of the five labels applies. A clear statement with no support for a label is no match.

Require an exact short quote from target for each label. Quote surrounding context only when it is essential to interpreting that target quote. Keep labels independent and include multiple labels only when each has specific evidence. When deciding between two labels, check what leverage the text actually uses. A threat can create fear without establishing a no-win choice. A moral accusation can discredit without pressuring someone to comply.
